# How to interview

## Listen, do not ask

You are not testing your hypotheses by asking about them. Ask people about their day, their process, the problem and the space. Then listen for whether what they describe confirms or contradicts a hypothesis.

- "Walk me through the last time that happened" beats "Would you use a tool that does X?"
- A story about what someone did counts for more than an answer to a direct question.
- Asking a hypothesis outright is the exception. If you do, note that the answer was prompted.
- Let silence sit. People fill it with the useful part.
- Do not pitch. If you describe your idea, do it at the end, and treat the reaction as weak evidence.

## Record it, with consent

Ask before you record. Say who will see it: your team, nobody else. Use a recording tool that gives you the full transcript, and bring it in with `/crm-sync`.

## Transcripts are verbatim

The transcript is the evidence. It goes into the hub whole, word for word.

- Never tidy, shorten or paraphrase a transcript.
- Keep speaker labels as the tool gives them. Do not guess names.
- If you only have notes, save them as notes and mark the transcript pending.
- Interpretation goes in the overview, never in the transcript.
- A quote in an overview is copied word for word from the transcript.

## No blame

Describe what people said they saw and did. Do not assign blame to anyone, especially staff or co-workers the person mentions. "They said nobody checked the orders for two hours" is fine. "The team ignored the orders" is not.

## Names and consent

Each person has a setting for whether they can be quoted by name. Check it before a name or a quote leaves the team, in a class presentation or anywhere else. When in doubt, use a role ("an operations manager at a mid-size company").

## After the interview

1. Run `/crm-sync` the same day, while you remember.
2. Write a short overview: what surprised you, and which hypotheses it touched, by number.
3. Log any follow-up with `/crm-outreach`.
